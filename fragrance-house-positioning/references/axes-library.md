# Axes library

A positioning map is only as good as its two axes. Pick dimensions that customers or the trade actually perceive, that you can support with evidence, and that vary *independently* across the houses you're mapping.

## Candidate dimensions

| Dimension | Low end → High end | Evidence you can cite |
|---|---|---|
| Sales volume and availability | Low volume / selective distribution → High volume / broad distribution | Number of stores or doors, retail channels, company reports, trade press on distribution |
| Brand positioning (prestige) | Accessible / everyday staple → Luxury / prestige | Parent-group classification, price tier, retail environment, trade-press coverage |
| Price tier | Mass → Masstige → Prestige → Luxury → Ultra-luxury | Retail price per 100 ml from official sites |
| Heritage | New entrant → Century-old house | Founding year from company history pages or reference works |
| Ownership | Independent / indie → Conglomerate-owned | Acquisition announcements, parent-company reports |
| Channel | Direct-to-consumer or direct selling → Wholesale (department stores, Sephora/Ulta, drugstores) | Company "where to buy" pages, annual reports |
| Scent identity | Safe crowd-pleasers → Avant-garde or artistic | Perfumer credits, note pyramids, trade reviews (harder to cite; use with care) |
| Customization | Standardized → Personalized or bespoke | Retail-concept coverage, brand service pages |
| Concentration mix | Mostly eau de toilette / body sprays → Mostly eau de parfum / extrait | Product catalogues |

## Picking a pair

1. **Choose one axis about reach and one about perception.** Volume/availability × prestige is the default because those two can separate: a house can be widely available *and* prestigious.
2. **Test for correlation before committing.** Place each house roughly on both axes. If they line up on a diagonal, the axes are measuring the same thing. Price × prestige almost always fails this test.
3. **Prefer citable dimensions.** "Scent identity" is real but hard to support with sources; use it only when the user wants a perception-driven map and accepts that placements will be argued, not cited.
4. **Match the question.** A focal firm deciding whether to expand distribution needs a volume axis. A firm deciding on price architecture needs a price axis. A firm worried about indie competition needs an ownership or customization axis.

## Wording the axes in the write-up

Fragrance leans toward luxury as a category, even at mass prices. Note this and define the prestige axis as relative to the other houses, for example: "The vertical axis measures each brand's positioning relative to the others rather than treating fragrance itself as strictly necessary or strictly luxurious."
