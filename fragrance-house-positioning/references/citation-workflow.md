# Citation workflow

The goal is that every factual claim in the analysis can be checked by a reader (often a professor) in under a minute. A wrong or unverifiable citation does more damage than a missing one, because it signals that the rest of the work might be careless too.

## 1. Find the claims that need a source

Cite:
- facts about a specific house: founding year, founder, ownership or parent group, number of stores, distribution model, retail concept, signature products, sales rank;
- market figures and trends: growth rates, market size, segment shares, "has grown in recent years";
- definitions or frameworks taken from a course text, lecture or assigned video.

Don't cite:
- the author's own placements, interpretations, recommendations and reflections;
- general reasoning (low volume raises unit costs) unless a figure is attached;
- common knowledge the audience wouldn't question.

Place the in-text citation right after the claim it supports. In a table cell, attach it to the specific bullet.

## 2. Find sources, best first

1. **Company primary sources**: annual reports, results press releases, official brand-history pages. These are best for distribution, ownership and financial facts.
2. **Trade and business press**: WWD, BeautyMatter, Business of Fashion, Vogue Business, Cosmetics Business, design publishers such as Gestalten.
3. **Industry data firms**: Circana (formerly NPD), NIQ, Euromonitor (often paywalled, so cite their public press posts).
4. **Reference works**: *International Directory of Company Histories* (via Encyclopedia.com), Britannica, and fragrance-industry bodies such as The Perfume Society.
5. **Course materials**: the textbook, lecture slides, assigned videos. Cite these when the user's assignment expects them.

Avoid: "market research" report mills that sell PDFs and publish teaser statistics with no method, SEO and content-farm blogs, AI-generated aggregators, social-media posts, and Wikipedia as a final citation (use it only to find primary sources).

## 3. Verify before citing

For every source:
- **Open it and read the relevant passage.** Search snippets and summaries can misstate what a page says.
- Confirm the passage supports the specific claim, not just the topic.
- Record exactly: author (or none), title, container (site or publication), publisher if different, publication date (or none), URL.
- If a page blocks automated fetching, try opening it in the browser. If it still can't be read, pick another source.
- PDFs (annual reports, press releases) can be downloaded and their text extracted to find the exact sentence and page.

If the best source only partly supports a claim (a report on luxury-fragrance growth cited for a claim about *niche* fragrance, for example), say so to the user. Offer to reword the claim to match the source or to keep looking. Don't silently stretch a source.

If you can't identify a source at all (an assigned video whose exact title or link is unknown, for example), leave it out and ask the user for it. Never guess a citation.

## 4. MLA 9 format

**In-text**
- Author's last name: (Seipp). Two authors: (Kotler and Armstrong). Add a page number for print or PDF sources when you have one: (Seipp 4).
- No author: a shortened title in quotation marks, in title case: (“Acqua di Parma”).
- Organization as both author and publisher (a company's own press release): MLA starts the entry with the title, so the in-text citation uses the shortened title: (“Revenue Exceeded”), not (Hermès).
- The citation goes inside the sentence, before the period or comma.

**Works Cited entry pattern**
`Author Last, First. “Title of Page or Article.” Container in Italics, Publisher (if different from the container), Day Mon. Year, URL. Accessed Day Mon. Year.`

- Include "Accessed …" when there's no publication date or the content changes.
- Drop `https://`. Keep URLs complete otherwise. Remove tracking or version query strings when the base URL still works.
- Abbreviate months longer than four letters: Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.
- Italicize containers (sites, journals, books, reference works). Put article and page titles in quotation marks.
- YouTube video: `“Video Title.” YouTube, uploaded by Channel Name, Day Mon. Year, www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID.`

**Works Cited page**
- New page at the end, titled "Works Cited", centered, not bold.
- Entries in alphabetical order by their first word, ignoring "A", "An" and "The".
- 0.5-inch hanging indent.
- Match the document's font and size. If the assignment requires strict MLA, the whole paper is double-spaced in a readable 12-pt font; mention this to the user rather than changing their whole document unasked.
