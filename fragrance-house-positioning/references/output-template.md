# Output template

Use this structure for a new write-up. Follow any assignment's own headings or length limit first; this template fills in what it leaves open.

```
<Title: e.g. "Positioning Map: Fragrance Houses">

Framework and Dimensions
  One or two sentences naming the houses and the two dimensions.
  One sentence on why prestige is measured relative to the other houses.
  Axis table: Axis | Dimension | Spectrum

<The map itself: a 2x2 chart or image, if the deliverable allows one>

Brand Positioning Analysis
  Table: Brand | Positioning Quadrant | Strategic Rationale & Considerations
    (rationale = 3–5 evidence bullets, then numbered implications)

What the Map Shows
  Competing strategies → the outlier → white space and why it may be empty → the marketing-mix point

Advantages of the Positioning Map   (bullets)
Limitations of the Positioning Map  (bullets)

What I Learned / Recommendations    (the user's own voice for a class reflection)

Works Cited                         (new page, MLA 9)
```

## Worked example

Five houses mapped on **sales volume and availability** (horizontal) × **brand positioning, accessible → luxury** (vertical).

| Brand | Quadrant | Evidence (cited) | Implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hermès | Low volume / High luxury | Craftsmanship; heritage; controlled distribution; association with a luxury house (“Revenue Exceeded”) | Prestige through exclusivity; sustainable while quality is protected and overexposure is avoided |
| Le Labo | Low volume / High luxury | Selective retail; personalized labels; distinctive store experience (De Souza Crook); a recognizable niche scent identity | Competes on individuality rather than reach; rapid expansion could weaken discovery and exclusivity |
| Acqua di Parma | Low volume / High luxury | Selective distribution; heritage; limited editions (“Acqua di Parma”) | Builds scarcity and an insider feeling among enthusiasts; growth limited by restricted awareness |
| Chanel | High volume / High luxury | Broad availability; strong recognition (Seipp); many category staples | Controlled presentation sustains scale without losing prestige; overexposure is the main risk |
| Avon | High volume / Accessible | Historically direct-to-consumer (“Avon Products, Inc.”); affordable; broad range | Differentiates on convenience and value; under pressure from other mass brands |

**What the map shows.** Hermès, Le Labo and Acqua di Parma compete on selective availability and prestige; Avon competes on accessibility and scale. Chanel is the outlier, combining broad distribution and high volume with a strong luxury association. The low-volume, accessible quadrant is empty among these houses. A small-batch, affordable house could try to enter it, but low production volume raises unit costs, which makes accessible prices hard to sustain. The market for luxury and prestige fragrance has grown in recent years (Jensen), which helps explain the attention on niche and indie houses.
