# Fragrance house archetypes

Use these to propose a competitor set that spans the market and to sanity-check placements. They describe typical patterns, not facts about any particular house. Every claim about a specific brand still needs its own source (see `citation-workflow.md`).

| Archetype | Typical position (volume × prestige) | What usually drives it | Main strategic risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heritage luxury maison (fashion or leather-goods house with a fragrance line) | Low–mid volume, high prestige | Craftsmanship, heritage, tightly controlled distribution, halo from the core luxury business | Overexposure and dilution if distribution widens too fast |
| Couture or designer house with blockbuster scents | High volume, high prestige | Iconic signature scents, heavy advertising, broad selective retail, strong recognition | Ubiquity eroding prestige; dependence on a few hero products |
| Niche or artisanal house | Low volume, high prestige | Selective retail, distinctive store concept, personalization, a recognizable scent identity | Rapid expansion (often after a conglomerate acquisition) weakening exclusivity and discovery |
| Indie perfumer or small-batch artist | Very low volume, mid–high prestige among enthusiasts | Artistry, transparency, direct communication, limited runs | Scale economics: high unit costs, thin distribution, founder dependence |
| Heritage regional house (e.g. an old Italian or French cologne maker) | Low volume, high prestige | Founding story, signature formula, limited editions, an "insider" community | Restricted awareness and growth |
| Mass-market or drugstore brand | High volume, accessible | Price, convenience, wide availability, body sprays and gift sets | Price competition, private labels and dupes |
| Direct-selling brand | High volume, accessible | Representatives, catalogues, value pricing, convenience | Channel decline; pressure from e-commerce and mass retail |
| Celebrity or influencer line | Mid–high volume, accessible to mid | Personal fame, launch hype, social media | Short product life cycles tied to the celebrity's relevance |
| Dupe or "inspired-by" brand | Mid volume, accessible | Low price for a recognizable scent profile | Legal and reputational risk; no proprietary identity |

## Building a good set

- Include at least three archetypes so the map has spread.
- Include one likely **outlier** (usually a couture or designer house with high volume and high prestige); outliers produce the most interesting analysis.
- If there's a focal firm, include its two or three closest direct competitors plus one or two contrasting houses.
- Watch for the **low-volume, accessible** corner. It's often empty, and the write-up should explain why (unit costs at low volume make low prices hard to sustain) before calling it an opportunity.
